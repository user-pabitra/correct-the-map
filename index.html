01 · The Resolution

What the General Assembly actually adopted

A non-binding resolution sponsored by Togo and backed by the African Union, adopted by recorded vote at the 114th plenary meeting of the 80th session on 4 September 2026.

164 in favour 1 against — United States 6 abstained — Estonia, Georgia, Lithuania, Rep. of Moldova, Serbia, Ukraine 22 not voting / absent

What it says

It calls for wider use of equal-area projections — naming Equal Earth — where comparative size matters, framing Mercator's inherited distortion as affecting education, culture, geopolitics and development narratives. The text describes correcting it as an act of “cognitive justice and memorial reparation”.

What it does not do

It is voluntary. Mercator is not banned; nothing becomes mandatory. The EU (via Ireland) stated on record that the text is limited to education, awareness-raising and geographic literacy and “neither addresses nor affects” sovereignty, territorial status or borders.

The abstentions, explained

Ukraine's envoy Andriy Melnyk abstained not over the principle but because reference Equal Earth maps depict occupied Ukrainian territory in a way he called “wholly unacceptable”, warning it could open a “Pandora's box”. Estonia, Georgia, Lithuania, Moldova and Serbia also abstained.

The objection on record

The United States cast the sole vote against. Ambassador Mike Waltz called the text part of a “much larger and more radical ideological project” and said such resolutions are “barnacles on our work”. The adopted text and certified voting record are the primary sources.

“A fair map does not change the geography of the world, it changes how we see the world.”
— Robert Dussey, Foreign Minister of Togo, introducing the draft
“It has to do with cognitive justice, with access to precise geographical knowledge, the dignity of peoples, historic memory.”
— Robert Dussey, on the campaign's purpose beyond cartography
02 · How We Got Here

457 years from navigation chart to UN vote

  • 1569Gerardus Mercator publishes his world chart. A straight line becomes a constant compass bearing — a navigational breakthrough whose geometry necessarily inflates area with latitude.
  • 1855–1973The equal-area argument goes public. James Gall's projection, revived by Arno Peters, forces the size-perception debate into classrooms — at the cost of heavily stretched shapes.
  • 1998National Geographic adopts Winkel Tripel for general reference maps, with mainstream cartography already drifting away from Mercator.
  • 2018Equal Earth is published by Bojan Šavrič, Tom Patterson and Bernhard Jenny in the International Journal of Geographical Information Science — an equal-area pseudocylindrical projection designed to look as natural as Robinson while preserving relative area exactly. Registered as EPSG:8857, implemented in PROJ as +proj=eqearth.
  • 2025“Correct The Map” campaign launches, led by Africa No Filter and Speak Up Africa, asking institutions to commit to equal-area world maps.
  • February 2026The African Union's 54 member states endorse the Equal Earth projection, converting advocacy into a continental negotiating position.
  • 4 September 2026The General Assembly adopts A/80/L.104, 164–1–6. Togo aims to update its school geography materials by the end of 2026.
03 · Projection Lab

See the distortion, measured — not asserted

Drag to interpolate between projections. Tissot indicatrices are computed by numerically differentiating the live projection: each ellipse began as a true circle on the globe. On an equal-area projection, every ellipse keeps the same area.

Mercator inflates area by exactly sec²φ

Mercator's scale at latitude φ is sec φ in every direction, so area grows by its square. Arithmetic, not opinion — and why Greenland (≈60–83°N) can rival a continent on screen.

05 · The Trade-off

No flat map can preserve everything

Gauss's Theorema Egregium settles it: a sphere cannot be flattened without stretching. Every projection chooses which property to keep. Equal Earth chooses to tell the truth about area.

ProjectionClassPreservesSacrificesIdentifier
Mercator (1569)Conformal cylindricalAngles, bearings, local shapeArea — sec²φ inflationEPSG:3395 · web 3857

Why Equal Earth, not Gall–Peters

Its authors built it after finding no equal-area world projection both accurate and visually acceptable to general audiences. It holds area exactly while keeping continental outlines close to their globe appearance.

Why Mercator survived

Rhumb lines plot straight — load-bearing for navigation — and simple square tiling made spherical Mercator the default of every slippy web map. The resolution leaves those uses untouched.

Why it matters

Relative size is how non-specialists read importance. If the base layer of geographic literacy systematically shrinks the Global South, correcting it is educational infrastructure, not decoration.

06 · Practitioner Toolkit

Adopting EPSG:8857 in a real stack

The resolution is a policy signal; this is the engineering behind it. Equal Earth has shipped in PROJ since 5.2 with an authoritative EPSG code — migration is a CRS decision, not a research project.

PROJ / QGIS
EPSG:8857 — "WGS 84 / Equal Earth Greenwich"
+proj=eqearth +lon_0=0 +datum=WGS84 +units=m +no_defs

# Pacific-centred variant (avoids splitting Asia)
+proj=eqearth +lon_0=150 +datum=WGS84 +units=m +no_defs

# QGIS: Project ▸ Properties ▸ CRS ▸ search "8857"
GDAL / OGR
ogr2ogr -t_srs EPSG:8857 countries_eqearth.gpkg countries_4326.gpkg
gdalwarp -t_srs EPSG:8857 -r bilinear -multi \
         -co COMPRESS=DEFLATE -co TILED=YES in.tif out_eqearth.tif
Python · GeoPandas / Cartopy
import geopandas as gpd, cartopy.crs as ccrs, matplotlib.pyplot as plt

gdf = gpd.read_file("countries.gpkg").to_crs(8857)
gdf["area_km2"] = gdf.geometry.area / 1e6   # never compute area in 4326 or 3857

ax = plt.axes(projection=ccrs.EqualEarth())
ax.set_global(); ax.coastlines(linewidth=.5)
Web · D3
const projection = d3.geoEqualEarth().fitSize([width, height], land);
const path = d3.geoPath(projection, context);
// Leaflet / MapLibre tiles remain EPSG:3857 — for equal-area web maps,
// render vectors yourself (D3, deck.gl, Observable Plot) or serve
// a reprojected image layer. Interactive globes sidestep projection.
Architectural rule of thumb: choose the CRS from the question. Area statistics and thematic world maps → equal-area (EPSG:8857). Navigation, bearings, tiled basemaps → Mercator. Country-scale distance or shape work → a local projected CRS. A single global default CRS is a smell, not a standard.
